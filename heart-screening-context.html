01Overview and positioning

A mobile app that records heart sounds from the chest, plus one recording from the neck beside the larynx, through a phone mic (or a ₹500 stethoscope + collar-mic attachment). It checks recording quality and uses AI to classify the heartbeat as Normal, Needs check-up or Recording unclear. One account can hold multiple patient profiles, each with its own screening history. Gemini explains the result in plain Hindi and English. The user can share the result with a doctor.

Rule #1: This is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. Never use the words "diagnose", "detects disease" or "you have X" in UI, API messages, prompts or the pitch. Say "screening", "pre-check", "may need a check-up".
  • Working name: not final. Shortlist: HridAI (recommended), Dhadkan, Spandan, LubDub. The submitted deck currently says "HeartSense". Repo code-name: heartscreen.
  • Problem: Heart disease is the leading cause of death in India. Rural areas have very few cardiologists, and AI digital stethoscopes cost ₹20,000+.
  • Target users: ASHA workers, PHCs and rural clinics running screening camps, families monitoring high-risk patients, doctors doing telemedicine.
  • Hackathon rule that matters: Using Google / Google Cloud tech and AI earns bonus marks. That is why we use Gemini, Firebase, Cloud Run and Vertex AI.
  • Competitor reference: Eko (US) has FDA-cleared murmur detection (87% sensitivity / 87% specificity) on its own digital stethoscope. Our angle: affordable, phone-first, Indian languages.

03Scope: MVP vs stretch

MVP (must ship for demo)

  • Multiple patient profiles per account: create, pick, edit, delete
  • Consent + patient details form (nickname or code, age, sex, height, weight)
  • Guided recording at 2 chest locations (mitral + aortic) and 1 neck location (beside the larynx), 15 s each
  • Server-side quality check with "re-record" feedback
  • Classification: Normal / Needs check-up / Recording unclear + confidence
  • Heart rate (bpm)
  • Gemini explanation in Hindi + English
  • Result screen with disclaimer; history list per patient
  • Deployed backend on Cloud Run

Stretch (only if MVP is done)

  • All 4 chest locations
  • Grad-CAM heatmap image on result screen
  • Upload old reports (image/PDF) → Gemini summary shown with result
  • Share result as PDF / link with doctor
  • Waveform playback with S1/S2 markers

Out of scope

  • Murmur type classification, ECG, on-device/offline model, doctor dashboard, clinical validation, any classifier or result based on neck audio. These go in the "future scope" slide only.

04Architecture

flowchart TD
  P[RN app: pick or add patient profile] --> A[RN app: consent + patient details]
  A --> B[RN app: guided recording per chest and neck spot]
  B -->|multipart upload| C[FastAPI on Cloud Run]
  C --> D{Quality check}
  D -->|rejected| B
  D -->|accepted chest| E[DSP preprocess: resample, bandpass, normalize]
  D -->|accepted neck| N[Stored only, not classified]
  E --> F[5 s windows to log-mel spectrograms]
  F --> G[Audio model: fine-tuned CardioPHON / CNN]
  A -.patient data.-> H
  G --> H[Optional fusion, off for MVP: audio score + age, sex, height, weight]
  H --> I[Result: normal / needs check-up / unclear]
  I --> J[Gemini: explanation in Hindi + English]
  J --> K[RN app: result screen, share, history]
  C <--> L[(Firestore + Cloud Storage)]

Key design decisions

  • No AI "enhancement" of audio. Denoising models can erase murmurs (murmurs look like noise) or invent beats. We use classical DSP only, and reject bad audio instead of fixing it.
  • Same preprocessing in training and inference. One shared Python module (audio.py) is imported by both the training code and the API.
  • Gemini never classifies. It only explains a result that our model already produced, and summarizes uploaded reports.
  • Neck audio is collected but never classified. The chest model has no labelled neck data, so its score on neck audio would mean nothing. Neck recordings get a quality check and are stored, but they do not affect the label, confidence, bpm or threshold.
  • Patient profiles sit under the account. One Firebase user owns many patients, and each screening belongs to one patient. A screening keeps a snapshot of the patient's details, so editing a profile later never changes an old result.
  • Backend owns all writes to Firestore and Storage. The app only talks to our API (simpler security rules).
  • Synchronous analyze call (a few seconds). No queues or workers for the hackathon.

07Recording spec (frontend)

  • Format: record with expo-audio high-quality preset: m4a / AAC, mono, 44.1 kHz. Android cannot record WAV natively, so we keep m4a on both platforms. Backend converts with ffmpeg.
  • Duration: target 15 s, hard minimum 8 s, max 30 s. Auto-stop at 15 s.
  • File size limit: 5 MB per recording.
  • Locations (chest codes are the same as the CirCor dataset; NK is ours):
    CodeNameWhere on body
    MVMitralLeft side, 5th rib space, below the nipple line (apex)
    AVAorticRight of breastbone, 2nd rib space
    PVPulmonicLeft of breastbone, 2nd rib space
    TVTricuspidLeft lower edge of breastbone, 4th–5th rib space
    NKNeckOne side of the neck, in the groove between the larynx (voice box) and the long neck muscle, at mid-neck height. This is over the carotid artery. Either side, one recording.
  • Neck safety: rest the phone lightly on the skin and never press hard. Firm pressure on the carotid artery can slow the heart and cause fainting, especially in older people. Show this on the placement screen with a "Stop if you feel dizzy" line, and let the user skip the neck step.
  • Source field: phone_mic or stethoscope_mic (user picks once on the placement screen).
  • User instructions, chest (show as 4 short tips with icons): sit still and breathe normally · switch off fan/AC/TV · press the phone's bottom (mic side) firmly on bare skin · don't talk during recording.
  • User instructions, neck (same style): sit still and breathe gently · switch off fan/AC/TV · rest the phone's bottom lightly beside the voice box, on bare skin · don't talk, swallow or clear your throat during recording.
  • Ask mic permission on screen 5, not at app launch. Handle "denied" with a settings link.

08API contract (v1)

Conventions

  • Base URL: EXPO_PUBLIC_API_BASE_URL (Cloud Run URL), all routes under /v1.
  • Auth header on every call except /healthz: Authorization: Bearer <Firebase ID token>. Backend verifies with firebase_admin.auth.verify_id_token. Users only see their own patients and screenings.
  • JSON keys in snake_case. Timestamps ISO 8601 UTC. IDs are strings.
  • Language: "en" or "hi". User-facing messages are returned in both (message_en, message_hi).
  • Mock mode: backend env MOCK_INFERENCE=true returns fixed realistic results, so the app can be built before the model is ready.

Cloud Storage

Security rules: deny all client reads/writes. Only the backend (Admin SDK) touches Firestore/Storage. Heatmaps are served through short-lived signed URLs.

The data bucket has soft delete off, so deleting a screening really deletes its audio, and a 30-day lifecycle rule on screenings/ as a backstop. Rejected recordings keep metadata only; their audio is deleted after the quality check.

Profiles stay until the user deletes them. The 30-day rule applies to audio, not to profiles.

Firebase Auth holds the login phone number. Our database never stores it, and no addresses are stored or full names asked for. The phone number stays out of logs and Gemini prompts. The only free-text identifier is the patient label, a nickname or code such as "Papa" or "Camp-12". The form hint says not to enter a full name, but a user can still type one, so treat label as personal data: it stays out of logs, Gemini prompts, screening snapshots and share text.

10Audio pipeline (backend)

One module, backend/app/services/audio.py, used by both the API and the training code. All numbers below are starting values; whatever is used in training must be used in inference. They live in one config object.

StepWhatStarting value
1. Decodeffmpeg → mono float32 PCM, from a temp file (not stdin)ffmpeg -protocol_whitelist file -t 35 -i in.m4a -ac 1 -f f32le pipe:1. Empty output counts as unsupported_audio.
2. TrimDrop first and last 1 s (handling noise when placing/removing phone)1.0 s each side
3. ResampleTo one target rate for all datasample_rate = 4000 Hz, CardioPHON's rate.
4. BandpassButterworth, zero-phase (scipy.signal.butter + sosfiltfilt), for quality checks onlyOrder 4, 25–400 Hz. The model gets unfiltered audio.
5. Spike removalRemove friction spikes (Schmidt et al. method)Optional
6. Quality checkSee below. Runs before normalizing, so level checks see the real signalReject → accepted: false
7. NormalizeScale amplitudeDivide by max abs value (peak normalize)
8. FeaturesLog-mel spectrogram of the whole recording, no windowsn_fft=512, hop_length=160 (40 ms), n_mels=64, fmin=60, fmax=2000; then log(S + 1.19e-7) and CardioPHON's fixed normalization

Quality check rules (all must pass)

  • Duration ≥ 8 s after trim → else too_short
  • Clipping: share of decoded samples with |x| ≥ 0.99 is under 1% → else clipping
  • Level: RMS of filtered signal, before normalizing, above floor (tune on real phone recordings) → else too_quiet
  • Noise: high energy at S1/S2 compared with between beats, low mains hum at 50/100/150 Hz, and low spectral flatness → else too_noisy. Don't use a 25–400 Hz energy ratio: fan hum and voice sit inside that band.
  • Heartbeat present: build an envelope (Hilbert or homomorphic), autocorrelate, find peak over 30–220 bpm. Weak peak → no_heartbeat_detected; rate outside 40–180 bpm → heart_rate_out_of_range. This also gives heart_rate_bpm.
  • CardioPHON's quality model is a separate classical classifier built at 1 kHz. Add it to quality.score only if the model trial shows it's usable.

Audio past 35 s is not decoded, which enforces the 30 s maximum without a new error code.

Tune thresholds on: dataset recordings marked good, dataset recordings marked unclear, and ~20 consented team phone recordings (quiet room vs fan on vs talking).

Neck recordings (NK)

  • Steps 1–6 run as normal, then processing stops. Steps 7–8 and the model are skipped.
  • Quality thresholds are stored per location type (chest vs neck). Chest values will not fit, because the neck adds breath sounds, swallowing and voice, and the pulse thump is strong at low frequency.
  • Tune neck thresholds on ~20 consented team recordings: at rest, breathing heavily, swallowing, talking. No dataset covers this, so until then keep the neck thresholds loose and log the outcome counts.
  • Mock mode needs a fixed bpm and quality result for NK.

Murmur Present / Absent / Unknown, detailed murmur labels, S1/S2 segmentation, age group, sex, height, weightPediatric (0–21 years, Brazil). Imbalanced: about 74% murmur absent, 19% present, 7% unknown. Check which split is publicly released.

Labels

  • Binary target abnormal: PhysioNet 2016 abnormal = 1, normal = 0. CirCor murmur Present = 1, Absent = 0. CirCor's label is per patient, so label recordings per site using the Murmur locations field.
  • CirCor Unknown: exclude from classifier training; use as negative examples for the quality check.

Splits (critical)

  • Split by patient, never by recording or window. Same patient in train and test gives fake high accuracy.
  • 70 / 15 / 15 train / val / test, stratified by label and dataset. Fix the random seed and save patient ID lists in ml/splits/.

Model plan

  1. Baseline first (day 1): small CNN on log-mel windows. Proves the pipeline end to end.
  2. Main model: fine-tune pretrained CardioPHON (self-supervised on 6 heart sound datasets, includes quality assessment). Run a one-day trial first to confirm its weights, licence, sample rate and input shape. If weights or code are hard to use in time, fine-tune a general pretrained audio CNN instead.
  3. Classifier: the checkpoint holds only the encoder, so we freeze it and train a small classifier in ml/notebooks/train_cardiophon_head.ipynb.
  4. Recording → screening: max over chest locations (favours sensitivity: one bad spot flags the screening). NK is excluded.
  5. Metadata fusion (late fusion): off for the MVP, so used_patient_data is false. CirCor covers ages 0–21, so adult inputs are extrapolation, and missing flags can act as a dataset identifier. Add it only if it beats audio-only on held-out data with missing flags removed. If used: logistic regression or small MLP on [audio_probability, age_years, sex, height_cm, weight_kg, bmi]. Map CirCor age groups to midpoint years and fill missing values with training medians. Report results with and without metadata.

Training details

  • Imbalance: class-weighted loss (or focal loss) + oversampling of abnormal windows.
  • Augmentation: random time shift, gain ±6 dB, added background noise at 10–30 dB SNR, small time-stretch (±5%), and a phone channel (high-pass, AAC round trip).
  • Early stopping on validation AUC.
  • Export: ONNX (model.onnx) for CPU inference with ONNX Runtime, plus model_meta.json holding the version, threshold and preprocessing. Version string like cardiophon-ft-0.1.
  • Explainability (stretch): Grad-CAM on last conv layer, overlay on spectrogram, save PNG. This needs torch, which is not in the runtime image, so precompute for demo samples or drop it.

Metrics to report (on test split)

  • Sensitivity, specificity, AUC, F1, confusion matrix, per dataset and combined.
  • Choose decision threshold on validation for sensitivity ≥ 0.85, on the final screening-level score using MV + AV only, and check the 1-recording case. Missing a sick heart is worse than a false alarm.
  • Log every run in ml/results.md: date, data, params, metrics.

Known limitations (say these in the pitch)

  • CirCor is pediatric; adult performance is unproven.
  • Training data is stethoscope audio; phone mic audio is different (domain gap).
  • Neither dataset has labelled neck recordings, so the neck recording does not affect the result. Say we collect it for future work; never claim the app assesses the neck or carotid artery.
  • No clinical validation yet.

16Privacy, safety, legal

  • Consent screen before any data is collected. Store consent version + timestamp.
  • Consent is per screening. When an ASHA worker or relative records someone else, that person (or their guardian) gives consent, not the app user.
  • Collect the minimum: a mobile number for login only, and no address, exact date of birth or full name. Profiles use a nickname or code.
  • Users can delete a screening (all audio and reports) or a whole patient profile (all of that patient's screenings).
  • Neck recording: light touch only, skippable, with a stop-if-dizzy warning (section 7). A doctor should review this instruction before real users.
  • A neck recording can also capture voice if the person talks. It gets the same 30-day limit and deletion rules as other audio.
  • Audio is kept for at most 30 days. Rejected recordings keep metadata only.
  • Get legal review before real users, covering minors' consent and Google's Generative AI health restrictions.
  • Follow the spirit of India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: clear purpose, consent, deletion on request.
  • Delete all hackathon data after the event unless the team decides otherwise.
  • Team test recordings: only from people who agreed, and never used to claim accuracy.
  • Wording: "screening", "pre-check", "may need a check-up". Never "diagnosis".

Consent text (draft, EN): "This app records sounds from your chest and neck and uses AI to check if your heartbeat sounds normal. It is a screening tool and does not replace a doctor. Your recordings and a saved profile (a nickname or code, age, sex, height, weight) are stored securely to give you a result and keep your history. Your mobile number is used only to log in. Please do not enter a full name in a profile. You can delete your data at any time."

20Glossary

TermMeaning
S1 / S2The two main heart sounds ("lub" and "dub"): valves closing at the start of systole and diastole.
Systole / diastoleHeart contracting / heart relaxing. Murmurs are described by which phase they occur in.
MurmurExtra whooshing sound from turbulent blood flow. Can be harmless or a sign of valve disease.
AuscultationListening to body sounds, usually with a stethoscope.
PCGPhonocardiogram: a recording of heart sounds.
LarynxThe voice box at the front of the neck. The Adam's apple is part of it.
Carotid arteryThe main artery up each side of the neck, beside the larynx. Our NK recording sits over it. Never press on it firmly.
Bandpass filterKeeps frequencies in a range (here 25–400 Hz) and removes the rest.
Mel-spectrogramA picture of sound: time on x, pitch on y, loudness as colour. The CNN reads this like an image.
SensitivityOf all truly abnormal cases, the share we flag. Our priority.
SpecificityOf all truly normal cases, the share we correctly call normal.
AUCHow well the model ranks abnormal above normal across all thresholds (0.5 = random, 1 = perfect).
Grad-CAMHeatmap showing which part of the spectrogram drove the model's decision.
Late fusionCombining the audio model's output with patient data in a second small model.
ASHA workerAccredited Social Health Activist: community health worker in India.
PHCPrimary Health Centre.
